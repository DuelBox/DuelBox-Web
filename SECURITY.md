# Security policy

## Reporting a vulnerability

**Open a private advisory:**
<https://github.com/DuelBox/DuelBox-Web/security/advisories/new>

Please do not open a public issue for a vulnerability. GitHub's private advisories let us
fix it and credit you before anyone else sees it, which is the whole reason this document
exists — a researcher with no route to report has publishing as their next best option.

If you cannot use GitHub advisories, say so in a public issue **without any detail** and we
will find another channel.

### What to send

Whatever you have. A single sentence naming the file and the problem is more useful than a
polished report that arrives a month later. If you can include the URL, the browser, and
either a proof of concept or the steps to reach the behaviour, that is ideal.

### What you can expect from us

| | |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgement | within **3 working days** |
| First assessment — in scope, severity, is it real | within **10 working days** |
| Fix or a dated plan | within **90 days** of the assessment |
| Credit | in the advisory and the release notes, unless you ask us not to |

We will tell you when the fix ships and we will not ask you to stay quiet indefinitely. If
we go past 90 days without a fix or a plan we have agreed with you, publish.

### Triage

Reports go to the repository owner, [@jatinsingh1603](https://github.com/jatinsingh1603),
who is the named owner for `packages/engine`, `packages/game-sdk` and everything under
`.github/` in [CODEOWNERS](.github/CODEOWNERS). Anything touching those, or anything scored
high or critical, is that person's to triage and cannot be delegated by default. A report
against a single game package may be handed to whoever is working on it.

## Scope

The shape of this product decides most of what is worth reporting.
[`docs/threat-model.md`](docs/threat-model.md) has the full reasoning; the short version is
that DuelBox is a static site with **no server runtime, no accounts, no database, and no
secrets**. There is no login to break, no session to steal, no query to inject and no
privilege to escalate.

### In scope

- Anything that runs code in a player's browser that they did not ask for — XSS, HTML
  injection, prototype pollution, unsafe deserialisation.
- A weakness in the [Content Security Policy](scripts/security-headers.mjs) or the header
  suite that makes one of the above reachable.
- Anything that lets one player see or alter the other player's state in a way the rules do
  not allow, in shared-screen or in cross-device play.
- Supply-chain problems: a dependency shipping something it should not, a build step that
  could be made to emit something it should not, a secret reaching the bundle.
- Anything that makes the origin serve content we did not build.
- Cache poisoning, or a route that can be made to serve the wrong content type.

### Out of scope

- **Missing headers on a host that is not ours.** The build emits `_headers`,
  `vercel.json` and server snippets; a deployment that ignores them is a misconfiguration,
  and we would still like to hear about it, but it is not a vulnerability in this project.
- **Denial of service by volume**, against a static origin. Rate limiting is the CDN's job.
- Reports generated by a scanner with no demonstrated impact — a header grade on its own,
  a version number in a fingerprint, `Content-Security-Policy` warnings about
  `'unsafe-inline'` in `style-src` (see the note in `scripts/security-headers.mjs` for why
  that one is deliberate).
- Self-XSS, or anything requiring the player to paste code into a console.
- Social engineering, physical access, or attacks needing a compromised device. Two people
  sharing one phone can already see each other's screen; that is the product.
- Missing `SameSite`, `Secure` or `HttpOnly` on cookies. There are no cookies.
- **Cheating in a local match.** A player who opens the console and edits their own score in
  a game they are playing on their own device has cheated at a game, not breached anything.
  Cross-device play is a different matter and is in scope.

## Safe harbour

If you make a good-faith effort to follow this policy, we will not pursue or support any
action against you for your research. Please stay within it: test against your own
browser and your own deployment, do not access or modify anyone else's data, and do not
degrade the service for other people.

## What we already do

- Every dependency advisory at **high** or above fails CI, on pull requests and nightly
  ([`.github/workflows/ci.yml`](.github/workflows/ci.yml),
  [`security.yml`](.github/workflows/security.yml)).
- Dependencies are updated by Dependabot with pinned action SHAs.
- The build refuses to ship a bundle containing anything shaped like a credential
  (`scripts/check-bundle-secrets.mjs`) and scans the source for the same
  (`scripts/check-source-secrets.mjs`).
- The header suite and a hash-based Content Security Policy are generated at build time
  from one source and asserted by a test, so a dropped header fails the build
  ([`scripts/security-headers.mjs`](scripts/security-headers.mjs)).
- No game may use `Math.random`, read `window`, `document`, `navigator` or wall time; lint
  enforces it.
